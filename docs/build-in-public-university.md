# Build in Public University: Hitchhiker's Guide to the Future

Status: publication-ready thesis draft. External publication requires Leo's approval of the exact destination and copy.

## The thesis

Your private thoughts are valuable and should be paid to access.

Not because every thought is correct. Not because privacy makes an idea profound. Private thinking is valuable when it contains the unfinished reasoning, observations, decisions, mistakes, and pattern-recognition that rarely survive public performance.

The Hitchhiker's Guide to the Future is being built around a simple proposition:

> A person should be able to set a price on access to their private thinking, let others enter by choice, and retain control over what becomes public.

A paid node is not a guru funnel. It is an accountable access relationship. The writer offers a living record of how they are trying to understand and change reality. The reader pays for proximity to that record. The writer is paid for making the thinking available, not for being right.

## The network thesis

Membership in one node can grant a traveler the right to create a node of their own. The network therefore grows through the conversion of readers into authors:

1. enter a node because the thinking is valuable;
2. read the private diary under the writer's terms;
3. write in a node of one's own;
4. publish selected updates that connect the work to observable reality;
5. contribute a distinct perspective to the Atlas;
6. become a useful waymark for other travelers.

The first network should not be maximally large. It should be seeded with valuable, somewhat prolific thinkers whose work opens different dimensions of reality: technical, cultural, institutional, personal, economic, artistic, scientific, local, and otherwise inconvenient.

This is curation, not hierarchy. The goal is not to rank people. The goal is to avoid building a semantic network in which every node is a slightly different room containing the same furniture.

## The grounding obligation

A private diary may contain speculation, intuition, and unfinished thought. That is allowed. It becomes more useful when the writer periodically publishes updates that connect the work to reality.

A grounding update should state:

- what the writer believed or was trying to build;
- what changed in the world, artifact, experiment, relationship, or institution;
- what evidence was observed;
- what the writer now believes;
- what remains uncertain;
- what would falsify the current view;
- what happens next.

The point is not to turn private thought into a polished personal brand. The point is to create a public trail between ideas and consequences.

## Suggested update rhythm

A node should publish a grounding update periodically, with the period chosen by the writer. The platform should support a suggested monthly rhythm without enforcing it.

Each update should be tagged as one of:

- observation;
- build receipt;
- experiment result;
- decision;
- correction;
- forecast;
- relationship or institutional signal;
- open question.

A missed update is not a moral failure. It is a missing measurement. The platform should show the absence honestly rather than manufacture activity.

## Public/private boundary

The platform does not require a writer to expose all of their private thinking. It requires a clear boundary.

- Diary entries are private by default.
- Public updates are explicit publications.
- Atlas inclusion is separately controlled.
- A public update may reference a private entry without exposing its body.
- A search result must explain why it is visible.
- Private writing is never used for global model training without separate affirmative consent.

The promise is paid access, not compelled disclosure.

## What we are building in public

The public build log will show:

- product and architecture decisions;
- shipped artifacts and their verification;
- cohort experiments and measured outcomes;
- failures, reversals, and corrections;
- the gap between the thesis and observed behavior;
- the parts of the system that should not yet be automated.

We will not publish private diary contents, private invitation reasoning, member identities without consent, payment secrets, or claims that are unsupported by receipts.

## Falsifiers

This thesis should be allowed to fail.

Evidence against it would include:

- people value public writing but will not pay for private access;
- paid readers do not return or use the private diary;
- members receive node-creation rights but do not write sustained nodes;
- writers price access but do not maintain a useful corpus;
- grounding updates become performative marketing rather than evidence-bearing connections to reality;
- Atlas search increases anxiety or surveillance concerns without producing meaningful discovery;
- the network converges on one narrow worldview despite deliberate seeding.

If these appear, the response is not to add more mythology. It is to publish the miss and change the product.

## Receipt requirements for future updates

Every public build update should link to:

- the repository revision or artifact;
- the exact experiment or decision it describes;
- the evidence class: internal process, external evaluation, public distribution, or empirical outcome;
- the falsifier or unresolved limitation;
- the next observable checkpoint.

A build update is not evidence merely because it is public. It is evidence when someone else can inspect the artifact and understand what changed.
